Executive Summary

Synthesized Argumentation

The present Special Leave Petition challenges the final judgment and order dated 14 July 2026 passed by the Division Bench of the High Court of Judicature at Bombay in Commercial Arbitration Appeal No. 104 of 2023. By the impugned judgment, the Division Bench upheld the dismissal of the Union of India’s Section 34 petition, thereby sustaining an arbitral award that unilaterally revised statutory royalty calculations under Mining Concession Agreement LX-901.

Dispute Genesis & Core Illegality

The Sole Arbitrator concluded that prolonged adverse weather in the 2021 mining quarters constituted a complete frustration of the revenue model, despite Clause 14.2 unambiguously stipulating fixed minimum royalties pegged to Indian Bureau of Mines (IBM) indices without weather amortizations. The Arbitrator substituted an equitable formula ex aequo et bono without authorization under Section 28(2) of the Act.

The statutory transgression falls squarely within Section 34(2A) patent illegality as illuminated in Associate Builders v. Delhi Development Authority (2015). The High Court erred critically in terming such contractual rewriting as a mere "plausible interpretation." It transgresses the foundational premise that an arbitrator is a creature of the contract and cannot travel beyond its explicit four corners.
